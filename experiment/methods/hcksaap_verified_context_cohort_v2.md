# hCKSAAP verified-context cohort amendment

The hCKSAAP_UbSite files provide 81-residue site windows. The released MMUbiPred checkpoint needs only a 49-residue window, but the residual hybrid's Long-Context Expert needs 257 residues from a full protein sequence. The original benchmark protocol required at least 95% exact UniProt context coverage before training.

The training-only feasibility audit, completed before model fitting or access to the published test split, found 12,194 preprocessed sites out of 12,199 raw sites. Exact coordinate and 49-mer validation retained 10,996 sites (90.1755%): 5,523 negative and 5,473 positive. Of the 1,198 excluded preprocessed sites, 987 had a published position beyond the retrieved protein sequence, 155 had a non-lysine at that position, 37 lacked a retrieved accession, and 19 had a 49-mer mismatch. The coordinate offset was unambiguous: 10,996 exact matches at offset 1 versus 6 at offset 0. Of 177 published `xN` isoform identifiers, 170 resolved to a UniProt isoform with at least one matching published 49-mer; 6 were not found and 1 did not match. An independent training-only audit found that a unique exact 49-mer search could rescue only 142 additional sites, including just 2 of the 987 out-of-range sites. It cannot restore 95% coverage.

The amended hCKSAAP study therefore retains the exact-coordinate, full-sequence-verified 257-residue cohort and lowers the **training** feasibility floor from 95% to 90%. It does not synthesize missing context, change coordinates, or use test labels for sequence resolution. Both newly trained experts use the same retained training subset so that their out-of-fold predictions and residual fusion are aligned. The original 95% configuration remains available as `hcksaap_residual_v1.json`; the amended configuration is `hcksaap_residual_verified_v2.json` and uses a separate run directory.

After the model and fusion rule are frozen, the published test split is processed with the same exact-match rule. Test coverage, class support, and exclusions are reported; no test-derived coverage threshold, model choice, or probability cutoff is introduced. The authors' released checkpoint and the residual hybrid are evaluated on identical retained test rows. This is a paired comparison on a **matched subset**, not a claim that both models were trained on identical full published datasets or that a test difference is attributable solely to architecture. Any manuscript table must state both training and test coverage alongside the metrics.
